# jobs.vision Founder-Level Conversion and Persuasion Audit

Audit date: July 28, 2026  
Scope: live public acquisition and pre-registration journey  
Lens: practice owner, eye-care job seeker, and skeptical first-time visitor

## Founder Verdict

jobs.vision looks like a real, focused product, but it does not yet make a compelling commercial case.

The strongest asset is the niche itself. “Eye care jobs, without the noise” is easy to understand, the domain is memorable, and the supported roles make the audience recognizable. The live job search reported **3,736 jobs**, which is meaningful marketplace proof.

The problem is that the homepage does almost nothing with that proof. It moves from a short slogan directly into pricing. Recruiters see what the site costs before they learn how many relevant candidates it reaches, why applicants will be better than those from a general job board, where the listing is distributed, what “enhanced visibility” means, or whether another eye-care practice has hired successfully.

Candidates are also asked to consider $20–$50 monthly plans before the site establishes that its listings are fresher, more relevant, or more useful than free alternatives. Most plan copy describes product features rather than a better job-search outcome.

The founder-level diagnosis is:

> **jobs.vision is selling access before it has sold belief.**

## Direct Answers

### Would I trust this site enough to spend $149?

**No, not today.**

The price itself is not the primary objection. One month of an unfilled manager role can cost a practice far more than $149. The problem is that the site gives me no credible basis for estimating a return:

- no candidate audience size;
- no recruiter results or case study;
- no employer testimonials or recognizable participating practices;
- no example of the listing I am buying;
- no explanation of distribution or “enhanced visibility”;
- no average views, applies, response rate, or time-to-fill;
- no explicit statement that the recruiter account is free;
- no clear explanation that the fee applies to one listing;
- minimal operator/support identity;
- sampled live listings previously led to closed application destinations, which directly weakens trust in marketplace quality.

I understand the amount, but I do not understand the expected value.

### Is it immediately obvious who the site is for?

**Yes at a category level.** Within five seconds, “Eye care jobs” and the role list communicate that this is for optometrists, opticians, technicians, managers, and front-office roles.

It is less clear that the site serves both job seekers and employers because the distinction is handled by a pricing tab rather than a strong two-sided value proposition.

### Is it obvious why someone should use jobs.vision instead of Indeed or LinkedIn?

**No.**

“Without the noise” hints at the correct differentiation—specialization and relevance—but the site does not explain or prove it. A visitor is left to invent the argument:

- more eye-care-specific jobs;
- fewer irrelevant applicants;
- better role taxonomy;
- faster matching;
- less recruiter screening;
- a community focused on optical and clinical hiring.

Those are potentially strong benefits, but the homepage does not state them.

### Does the homepage communicate benefits or merely features?

**Mostly features.**

The only clear outcome language is:

- “without the noise”;
- “Find jobs”;
- “Hire faster”;
- “without overspending.”

The cards then switch to features: saves, maps, alerts, badges, visibility levels, listing duration, and renewal. There is no proof that these features produce better candidates, faster hiring, earlier discovery, or a better job.

### Where do users hesitate?

#### Recruiters

1. “Why should I pay this niche site instead of posting where I already post?”
2. “How many relevant candidates will see my job?”
3. “What does enhanced or strongest visibility actually include?”
4. “Why does a manager listing cost more than a staff listing?”
5. “Is this a recruiter-account subscription or a fee for one job?”
6. “What exactly renews?”
7. “Why is the first 30 days more expensive than later renewals?”
8. “Can I edit the listing? Where do applicants go? What happens after I pay?”
9. “Who runs this company, and can I get help if something goes wrong?”
10. “Has another practice hired successfully here?”

#### Candidates

1. “Are these jobs fresher or more complete than jobs I can find for free?”
2. “Why should I pay $20 or $50 when major job boards are free?”
3. “Who sees a featured candidate profile?”
4. “How much faster are these alerts?”
5. “Will employers actually search this candidate database?”
6. “Can I browse and apply without paying?”
7. “Why did my selected Plus plan disappear when I reached registration?”

#### Skeptical visitors

1. “Is this an established marketplace or an attractive early-stage site?”
2. “How many jobs, employers, and active candidates are here?”
3. “Are listings verified and current?”
4. “Who owns and operates the service?”
5. “Why should I trust its billing or data handling?”

### Where is confidence lost?

- The homepage jumps from slogan to price without a proof section.
- Recruiter plans use vague visibility labels rather than concrete deliverables.
- A selected $149 plan is not carried into recruiter registration.
- “Create Recruiter Account” appears after a recurring price without saying the account is free.
- Candidate paid-plan intent is also lost at registration.
- The Contact page consists of one support email.
- The Terms explain renewal and non-refund behavior only after the commercial decision.
- Marketplace freshness is not presented as a promise, and sampled closed jobs undermine it.

### Is recruiter pricing explained clearly?

**No.**

The cards provide visible amounts:

- Staff: $79 first month, $49/month after;
- Manager: $149 first month, $99/month after;
- Doctor: $299 first month, $199/month after.

They also say “30-day listing,” “Renews monthly,” and “Cancel anytime.” This suggests listing-based billing, but it never explicitly states:

> “Recruiter accounts are free. You pay only for each job listing.”

The next screen is titled “Create Recruiter Account” and contains no selected listing or price. That makes the account/listing distinction materially ambiguous.

### Is renewal pricing communicated appropriately?

**The numbers are visible, but the explanation is not commercially complete.**

The copy does not answer:

- Does the same listing remain active?
- Is renewal automatic every 30 days?
- When must cancellation occur?
- Why is the first period more expensive?
- Does the first period include a launch boost or setup work?
- Is a renewal charge refundable?

A clearer formulation would be:

> **$149 for the first 30 days. If you keep this Manager Job Listing live, it automatically renews every 30 days at $99. Cancel before the next renewal. Your free recruiter account is never charged separately.**

If the initial period includes additional promotion, say so. If it does not, the price difference feels arbitrary.

### Can perceived value increase without adding features?

**Yes—substantially.**

The existing product can feel more valuable by presenting:

- 3,736 live jobs as marketplace proof;
- number of eye-care employers or locations represented;
- recent job examples;
- a sample recruiter listing;
- a clear explanation of role-specific targeting;
- what existing search filters reveal about the niche;
- specific definitions of visibility levels;
- the cost of a vacant chair compared with the listing price;
- a freshness/verification promise;
- a simple general-board comparison;
- a founder/category story;
- clear account and billing language.

Most of the conversion opportunity is currently messaging, packaging, and proof—not additional functionality.

## Persona Audit

### 1. Optometrist or practice owner

#### First 5–10 seconds

The category relevance is strong. I know the site understands eye care and can see a recruiter path.

#### Psychological state

I am probably not casually browsing. I have an uncovered schedule, an overworked team, lost appointments, or a growth plan being delayed by hiring. My question is not “Does this website have a job-posting form?” It is:

> “Will this put my role in front of qualified eye-care people quickly enough to be worth $149?”

The homepage does not answer that.

#### Purchase resistance

The price cards ask me to trust “enhanced visibility” without defining the audience or distribution. Role-based pricing may also feel like value-based price discrimination unless the increased reach is concrete.

#### What would persuade me

- qualified eye-care audience size;
- real employer result;
- example listing;
- estimated or typical exposure;
- concrete distribution;
- fresh/verified marketplace promise;
- clear support and refund/extension policy;
- explicit free-account/per-listing statement.

### 2. Optician or technician

#### First 5–10 seconds

The role chips make me feel included. That is a genuine strength.

#### Psychological state

I want relevant local jobs, accurate pay and scheduling information, current openings, and the shortest path to applying. I am wary of paying for job search because free alternatives are abundant.

#### Registration resistance

The site promotes paid candidate tiers before explaining why its inventory is uniquely useful. “Unlimited saves,” “map search,” and “email alerts” sound like software features, not a better career outcome.

#### What would persuade me

- “Browse 3,736 eye-care jobs free”;
- fresh jobs checked regularly;
- examples in my role/location;
- no irrelevant healthcare categories;
- optional paid tiers positioned as speed and convenience, not access;
- clear proof that employers see or search featured profiles.

### 3. Skeptical first-time visitor

#### First 5–10 seconds

The site looks focused and legitimate enough to explore, but not established enough to purchase from without further evidence.

#### Trust gap

The homepage contains no company story, marketplace statistics, employers, testimonials, operating identity, support expectation, or explanation of how listings are sourced and kept current.

#### Likely conclusion

“Interesting niche job board; I need to verify whether anyone actually uses it.”

## Business Positioning

### Current implied position

> A visually focused eye-care job board with role-specific listings and optional search/posting upgrades.

### Stronger position

> **The hiring marketplace built only for eye care—so practices reach relevant candidates without screening through unrelated applicants, and eye-care professionals find the right role without searching a general job board.**

This creates a shared two-sided promise: **relevance saves time**.

### Suggested core messaging architecture

#### Hero

**Hire eye-care talent without sorting through the wrong applicants.**

Reach optometrists, opticians, technicians, managers, and front-office professionals in one focused marketplace.

Primary recruiter CTA: **Post a Job — accounts are free**  
Secondary CTA: **Browse 3,736 Eye-Care Jobs**

Candidate variant:

**Find your next eye-care job without searching a general job board.**

Browse thousands of roles for optometrists, opticians, technicians, managers, and practice teams.

#### Proof bar

- 3,736 current eye-care jobs
- [Verified number] employers/practice locations
- Five focused eye-care role categories
- Free candidate and recruiter accounts

Only publish metrics that can be maintained accurately.

#### Differentiation section

| General job board | jobs.vision |
|---|---|
| Broad audience | Eye-care-only audience |
| More irrelevant discovery | Role-specific search and targeting |
| Generic job taxonomy | Optometry, optical, technician, manager, and front-office paths |
| Hiring volume story | Relevance and less screening |

Avoid attacking competitors. Make the category advantage explicit.

## Prioritized Recommendations

### R1 — Lead with marketplace proof before pricing

- **Recommendation:** Put the live job count, employer/location count, recent roles, and a freshness statement above the pricing section.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Buyers use activity as a proxy for safety. Marketplace liquidity reduces the fear of paying into an empty room.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S

### R2 — Explicitly separate free accounts from paid listings

- **Recommendation:** Add “Recruiter accounts are free. Pay only for each job listing.” above every recruiter price and repeat it during registration.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** It removes loss aversion created by an ambiguous recurring account fee.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS

### R3 — Explain exactly what the $149 Manager listing buys

- **Recommendation:** Define listing duration, editability, application routing, distribution, visibility placement, support, and renewal in concrete language.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Specific deliverables make an unfamiliar product feel controllable and comparable.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S

### R4 — Rewrite the hero around the pain of irrelevant applicants

- **Recommendation:** Replace the generic supporting line “Find jobs. Hire faster.” with a specific two-sided outcome.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Specific pain recognition signals category expertise and creates emotional relevance.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS

Suggested recruiter copy:

> Reach eye-care professionals without sorting through unrelated applicants.

Suggested candidate copy:

> Search roles built around your eye-care career—not a generic healthcare feed.

### R5 — Preserve the selected product and price through registration

- **Recommendation:** Carry Manager/Staff/Doctor or Plus/Premium into registration and show “No charge today; review before checkout.”
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Continuity confirms that the site understood the user’s decision and prevents suspicion that the price or product may change.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S

### R6 — Add a recruiter trust stack

- **Recommendation:** Add a real employer result, example listing, support promise, operator identity, Stripe/payment reassurance, and job-quality/freshness policy.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Trust is cumulative. Several small, verifiable signals reduce the perceived risk of buying from an unfamiliar brand.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S–M

### R7 — Reframe pricing around vacancy economics

- **Recommendation:** Place the listing price beside the cost of delay: unfilled appointments, staff overtime, owner time, and lost growth. Avoid invented statistics.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** $149 feels expensive when compared with “posting a form” and inexpensive when compared with one more week of operational pain.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS

Example:

> One unfilled manager role can cost far more than the listing. Reach a focused eye-care audience for $149 for the first 30 days.

### R8 — Clarify and justify the lower renewal price

- **Recommendation:** Say what renews, when, at what price, and why renewal costs less. If the first period includes launch promotion, name it.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Unexplained price mechanics feel arbitrary; transparent mechanics feel fair.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS

### R9 — Show a recruiter listing preview

- **Recommendation:** Add “See an example listing” beside the purchase CTA.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Preview reduces uncertainty and lets a buyer mentally own the outcome before paying.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** S

### R10 — Make the free candidate account the default conversion

- **Recommendation:** Lead with “Browse jobs free” and “Create a free candidate account”; present Plus/Premium as optional accelerators after value is established.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Job seekers are highly price-sensitive. A low-risk first commitment builds habit and supplies the recruiter-side audience.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S

### R11 — Convert candidate features into outcomes

- **Recommendation:** Rewrite every feature as a job-seeker result.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Candidates buy progress, speed, and confidence—not saves, maps, or badges.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** XS

Examples:

- “Map search” → **See which opportunities fit your real commute.**
- “Email alerts” → **Know when a matching role opens.**
- “SMS alerts” → **See high-fit roles before your next browsing session.**
- “Unlimited saves” → **Keep every promising opportunity organized.**
- “Priority profile placement” → **Appear earlier when participating employers browse candidates.**

Only promise employer discovery if the product truly supports it.

### R12 — Explain why jobs.vision wins against general boards

- **Recommendation:** Add a short comparison focused on relevance, specialization, and reduced screening.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** A new brand must answer the default-option question. If it does not, users retreat to the familiar incumbent.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** S

### R13 — Add proof immediately beside recruiter prices

- **Recommendation:** Put one verified result, marketplace metric, or employer quote directly above/below pricing.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Evidence at the decision point is more persuasive than evidence on a separate About page.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS–S

### R14 — Define “basic,” “enhanced,” and “strongest” visibility

- **Recommendation:** Replace relative labels with concrete placement/distribution.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Vague superlatives create suspicion and make tier comparison impossible.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** XS

### R15 — Introduce risk reversal

- **Recommendation:** Consider an honest guarantee, such as an extension if a paid listing receives no qualified engagement, after validating the economics.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** The recruiter’s fear is not the $149 charge; it is paying and hearing nothing.
- **Expected impact:** High
- **Effort:** M

### R16 — Add a founder/category story

- **Recommendation:** Explain why jobs.vision exists and what the team understands about eye-care hiring.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** A credible origin story converts “small unknown site” into “specialist built by people who understand my problem.”
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** XS–S

### R17 — Strengthen Contact and commercial reassurance

- **Recommendation:** Identify the operating business, support response expectation, billing help path, and location/jurisdiction when appropriate.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Buyers look for signs that a real, accountable operator will answer after payment.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** XS

### R18 — Rename recruiter products as job listings

- **Recommendation:** Use “Staff Job Listing,” “Manager Job Listing,” and “Doctor Job Listing,” not merely “Position.”
- **Why it matters psychologically:** The billing object becomes concrete and less likely to be confused with an account plan.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** XS

### R19 — Add a low-commitment recruiter CTA

- **Recommendation:** Keep “Post a Job” but add “See how it works” or “Preview a listing.”
- **Why it matters psychologically:** Skeptical visitors are not ready for the highest-commitment action immediately after a slogan.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** S

### R20 — Merchandise the existing niche filters as expertise

- **Recommendation:** Explain that candidates can search by eye-care role, setting, employment type, brand preference, radius, and specialized OD criteria.
- **Why it matters psychologically:** These existing capabilities demonstrate that the site understands the category better than a general board.
- **Expected impact:** Medium
- **Effort:** XS

1. Lead with marketplace proof, including the current job count.
2. State that recruiter accounts are free and billing is per listing.
3. Explain exactly what each recruiter listing includes.
4. Rewrite the hero around relevant applicants and focused eye-care careers.
5. Preserve plan and price context through registration.
6. Add recruiter outcome proof directly beside pricing.
7. Clarify and justify renewal timing and price.
8. Make free candidate registration the primary candidate conversion.
9. Explain the niche advantage over general job boards.
10. Add a listing preview and lower-commitment recruiter CTA.

## Top 5 Trust Improvements

1. Publish accurate marketplace activity and freshness metrics.
2. Add verified employer outcomes, testimonials, or case studies.
3. Explain how listings are sourced, checked, and removed when closed.
4. Identify the operator and set a support response expectation.
5. Put billing, renewal, cancellation, refund, and Stripe reassurance beside the purchase decision.

## Top 5 Copy Improvements

1. **“Recruiter accounts are free. Pay only for each job listing.”**
2. Replace “Find jobs. Hire faster.” with a specific relevance-based outcome.
3. Replace “basic/enhanced/strongest visibility” with concrete deliverables.
4. Rewrite candidate feature bullets as job-search outcomes.
5. Replace “first month / mo after” with “first 30 days / each 30-day renewal.”

## Biggest Missed Opportunity

The site already has the ingredients of a defensible category story—**a memorable domain, a focused eye-care audience, specialized role filters, and thousands of jobs—but the homepage presents none of that as proof of marketplace strength.**

The biggest opportunity is not adding another feature. It is turning existing inventory and specialization into a clear promise:

> **For practices: fewer irrelevant applicants. For eye-care professionals: fewer irrelevant jobs.**

That is a stronger reason to choose jobs.vision than “another place to post or search.”

## Would I Spend My Own Money Today?

**No.**

I would explore the site and might create a free account, but I would not spend $149 yet because the site does not show enough evidence that a Manager Job Listing will reach qualified candidates or produce a credible return. The account/listing distinction and renewal mechanics also require too much inference.

I could become willing to buy without a major product rebuild if the site:

1. fixed and proved listing freshness;
2. stated that accounts are free and fees are per listing;
3. showed the relevant audience and marketplace activity;
4. defined what the listing receives;
5. presented one credible employer outcome;
6. carried my selected $149 product through registration;
7. explained renewal and cancellation in plain language.

At that point, $149 would feel reasonable because the cost would be framed against an expensive hiring problem rather than against the act of publishing a web page.
